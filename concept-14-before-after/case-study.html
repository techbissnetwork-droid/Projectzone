The Before

Harlow & Finch Retail runs four home-goods stores across the region. Until last year, every one of them kept its own paper trail. Deliveries were checked against a clipboard and signed off by hand; invoices went into a binder behind the counter, one per supplier, and stayed there unless someone needed to dig one out to settle a dispute.

Stock counts happened once a week — Friday evenings, after close, when staff walked the floor with a printed sheet and counted every SKU by hand. The numbers got phoned or emailed to head office by Monday morning, which meant that for most of the week, nobody actually knew what was on the shelves. A lamp that sold out in Store 2 on Tuesday could still show as "in stock" when Store 4 promised it to a customer on Thursday.

Worse, the four stores couldn't see each other at all. Moving inventory between locations meant a phone call and a guess, not a lookup. Reordering was gut feel — a manager's sense of what "usually" sells — which produced the familiar mismatch of empty shelves in the busy aisles and a back room quietly filling up with stock nobody was tracking.

The Approach

We didn't try to fix everything at once. Over an 8-week build, we replaced the paper trail with one connected system — store by store, piece by piece.

Built one central inventory system

Every SKU, every store, one database. Stock levels update the moment something is sold, received, or moved between locations — no end-of-day sync, no waiting for Friday.

Connected all 4 store locations

Each till and stockroom now writes to the same live system, so a manager in Store 1 can see exactly what Store 4 has on the shelf, right now.

Built a simple staff-facing app

Replaced the clipboard with a phone-friendly app for receiving deliveries and running spot-counts — no manual, no separate head-office login.

Migrated three years of paper records

Digitized the old invoice binders so nothing was lost in the switch — supplier terms, sales history and reorder patterns all carried over.

The After

Today, all four Harlow & Finch stores run off the same live inventory system. A delivery gets scanned in and it's visible everywhere within seconds — not by Monday, not after a phone call, immediately. Staff run quick counts during a slow afternoon instead of losing a Friday evening to a clipboard.

Managers can see what every other store has on the shelf, so a customer asking for something that's sold out locally can usually still get it — transferred in from three miles away instead of losing the sale. Head office gets a real picture of what's moving, store by store, instead of a Monday-morning guess built from four phone calls.
