Web Applications

Not a website. A tool your team opens every day.

We build the internal tools, portals and platforms that replace the spreadsheets, group chats and sticky notes holding your operations together — shaped around how your business actually runs, not a generic template.

What we build

Four kinds of tools, one job: giving your team something they can trust.

Most businesses end up running some mix of these — often more than one at a time.

01

Internal Dashboards & Tools

One shared view of inventory, jobs or numbers, instead of five spreadsheets nobody trusts equally.

Before: five tabs open, all slightly different. After: one dashboard everyone trusts.
02

Customer Portals

A place your customers log in to check orders, download documents, or see their own account status.

Before: "can you email me that again?" After: they just log in and check.
03

Booking & Scheduling Systems

Real-time calendars that stop double-bookings before they happen, synced across your whole team.

Before: two calendars, one double-booking. After: one calendar, zero conflicts.
04

Custom SaaS-Style Platforms

Multi-user platforms with logins, roles and permissions — built the way a real product is built.

Before: an idea on a whiteboard. After: a platform people log into daily.

Questions

Questions we hear before every application project.

Both live on the web, but they do different jobs. A website is public — anyone can land on it. A web application is where your team, or your customers, log in to actually do something: manage jobs, check status, update records. Most businesses end up with a bit of both — a public site plus a private tool behind it — and we build them to work together.

In most cases, yes. We regularly connect new tools to accounting software, payment processors, calendars, email and other systems you already rely on. We map out what needs to talk to what during discovery, before a line of code gets written.

Software isn't "done" the day it launches — your business keeps changing, and the tool should too. We offer ongoing support plans for updates, new features and monitoring; see Pricing for how that's packaged, or we can scope changes individually as they come up.

Role-based permissions so people only see what they're supposed to, encrypted connections on every page, and hosting on monitored, access-controlled infrastructure — the same security backbone described on our SSL & Business Email page, applied to your application as well as your site.
